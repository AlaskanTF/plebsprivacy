My View: Why Singaporeans are Losing Motivation

September 8, 2026 AlaskanTF(ATF)
Reflections

I am an idiot who likes to post nonsense but this is my opinion and view, POFMA if it's true.

For years, probably decades, the "Singaporean Dream" was:

Study hard, go to a local university, get a decent job, and you'll be able to afford a house/BTO, eventually own a car, build a family and retire securely.

You were never promised that you would become rich.

For generations, that appeared true.

Today, that's just complete bullshit. That contract as long expired. Housing is more expensive, cars are fuck off expensive, everyday costs have risen, career competition has intensified and climbing the career ladder does not produce the same kind of improvement to quality of life compared to the previous generation.

Looking at Singapore now, we get a sense of apathy, burnout and quiet quitting. Many are opting out or looking to opt out of the traditional rat race, wondering what's the point of climbing the corporate ladder that keeps sinking or never ends.

While skyrocketing prices are always to blame, weather its housing price, COE price or core inflation, a major driver that have never, or maybe seldom talked about in mainstream media is: cross-border labour arbitrage which I would call the "Currency Multiplier Effect" and a system built on the quiet, systemic suppression of local wealth.

1. The Multi-National Talent Pipeline 

When discussing the labour force in Singapore, we have the Singaporeans and the multi-national talent pipeline spanning from Malaysia, China, Thailand, Myanmar Indonesia, Philippines, India, Bangladesh and many others.

What unites this foreign pipeline is a single economic superpower I mentioned earlier: the currency multiplier effect.

The Math of the Multiplier for foreigners vs. The Local Dead End

I'm not going to use some bullshit stats about fresh grads earning 4.5k out of university that has been reported. I will using what I am seeing in real life among the average citizen. I will consider the baseline entry-level job paying SGD3,000 a month. To a Singaporean trying to survive locally on a SGD3,000 a month. Oh sorry, SGD2,400 after CPF.

Factoring in core inflation, food, public transport and the rising cost of renting a room because who the fuck wants to stay with their parents forever and because you can't get a BTO yet, you are left with a salary that leaves you with zero breathing room. Simple put it: FUCKED.

Now, we look at that exact same SGD3,000 salary through the eyes of a regional worker from the multi-national talent pipeline.

  • The Foreign Worker's Exit Strategy: The regional worker can come to Singapore, live frugally (splitting cost of rent) and saving aggressively. If they work here for 6 to 7 (SIXXX SEVEN), their accumulated savings - multiplied heavily upon conversion back to whatever their local currency is, their capital would be able to buy them a house and car (not a lease, but OWNED) fully paid in cash. They return home as upper middle class establishment owners.
  • The Local's Reality: A Singaporean earning that same SGD3,000 has no way to take advantage of the currency multiplier effect. They are stuck right here. For them, a SGD3,000 salary means never being able to comfortably afford a car (thank you COE) or keep pace with surging private property prices. They are locked in a permanent financial treadmill hooked up to an unlimited power supply source.

 2. The Multiplier Effect Cycle: A One Way Street

Let's take a look at the macro-level loop why The Multiplier Effect Cycle is a one way street to help us fully understand why this system breeds burnout, quiet quitting and dispair.

Global labour migration isn't just about individual choice but is driven by the difference in strength of currency.

  • The Upward Migration Loop: Workers from countries with weaker currencies have a clear upward escape hatch. They can move to a strong currency hub like Singapore, extract the strong currency, and take it back home to multiply its purchasing power exponentially.

Home country -> Singapore -> Earn SGD -> Save SGD -> return home -> Convert SGD to home currency (3x-10x) -> higher purchasing power

  • The Trapped Local Population: In contrast, Singapore's have no upwards escape hatch and no way to experience the multiplier effect. A Singaporean cannot move to a weaker currency country, earn a localized salary and expect it to magically multiply their purchasing power back home. Do so, they would be met with a fuck you pay cut and immediately drop their standard of living.

Local earn SGD -> 1x purchasing power and depressed salary

3. When the Citizens Are the Commodity: The Ultimate Resource

As we all know, Singapore's greatest vulnerability, Singapore has fuck all in resource, no oil, no land, no agriculture. When you look and analyse how the economic machine actually operates. You see the reality. Similar to: If it's free, you're the product. In a country with no natural resources, the workers are the resource.

Just like any other industrial input in a super competitive hub, resources are optimized to be as efficient, cost effective and productive as possible for maximum output.

The Nation State as a Corporate Balance Sheet

If a country's primary economic engine relies on being a financial hub for high margin multinational corporations (MNCs), keeping labour cost as low as possible while maintaining efficiency and productivity is priority #1. If local salary rise too aggressively, these MNCs would face a margin compression and look somewhere else.

To prevent this, the system has to maintain an elastic, multinational labour pipeline where workers whose baseline expectations are anchored by much weaker home currency, this way the market achieves continues salary containment.

  • For the Macro Economy: GDP grows, FDI (Foreign direct investment) flows in, and Singapore remains a financial/economic powerhouse on paper.
  • For the Micro Citizen: Local workers find themselves competing against and endless labour supply willing to accept lowered/compressed salary because of currency multiplier.

If you actually stop, look and think, wealth and salary suppression is done on purpose, to keep the macroeconomic machine running smoothly.

4. How Wealth Suppression Destroy Local Mobility

As regional workers eyes the massive currency multiplier effect, they are structurally capital of accepting lower salary than locals who must fund a 100% domestic lifestyle without the currency multiplier effect or an upward escape hatch.

For Businesses/MNCs operating in Singapore, this creates an endless supply of labour willing to accept lower salary for entry level to mid level jobs. Companies would naturally adjust their salary range downwards. However, while salary drop to match migrant labour seems easily tolerated, locals are effectively priced out of true wealth accumulation.

All locals can do is watch their foreign peers use Singapore as a multi year "wealth extraction machine" to buy a home (not lease), car (not just 10 years), start their own business back home. While, the local working the exact same desk jobs cannot even afford basic independence or a car locally. Wealth building for the locals are effectively bottlenecked.

The "Wealth Suppression" Hypothesis

As mentioned, Singapore's only resource is human capital and labour force. Singapore depends heavily on a large share of the population remaining productive in the work-force, particularly high value MNCs and domestic firms.

If a large proportion of locals accumulated enough wealth to become financially independent, that would change the relationship between labour and capital. Workers would have greater bargaining power to reject jobs, work fewer hours, take career risk (starting a business), retire early or leave the work force permanently. MNCs would have to raise salary and have difficulty retaining talent. This would lower the efficiency of the MNC, potentially compressing the margins. Government would collect less corporate tax, income tax and receive less economic output.

Thus, it's in the interest of the government to ensure slow wealth accumulation or at least keep wealth illiquid (CPF) to keep locals economically dependent on employment for longer. Keeping housing prices high, COE and retirement requirements. All these are just to keep you dependent on employment through debt. All these are strategically put in place to ensure the high cost of opportunity of leaving the workforce all increase the amount of wealth one needs before they can leave the workforce or opt out of the rat race.

The result of this system is locals feeling stuck in place and unable to progress while being dependent on continued employment. They have assets and productive wealth but do not possess enough liquid assets to be genuinely be financial free.

5. From Economic Pressure to Existential Apathy

All these changes the human psychology. Motivation requires a basic belief in cause and effect (where effort leads to tangible rewards).

  • The Asset & Housing Wall: When local salary is suppressed by labour supply dynamics while cost of living skyrockets due to global capital inflows, locals realizse that saving a standard local salary would not bridge the gab.
  • The Loss of Agency: Locals feel everything stacked structurally against them. Motivation, ambition and looking forward to the future turns into total cynicism.

Foreigner workers use Singapore as temporary, high yield incubator to extract and build their wealth back home. But the locals, who are the permanent population of this resource are expected to fuel the nation's economy output while being structurally prevented from building generational wealth of their own.

I am not pointing fingers at foreign workers who are simply making the rational choice to secure their and their families futures. If there was a country directly linked to Singapore with a causeway that has a stronger currency, the rational choice would be to head over there to work and take advantage of the currency multiplier effect.

So the question of the day is: Why break your back carrying a system that ensures you always stay where you are or behind?

Fruit for thought.
