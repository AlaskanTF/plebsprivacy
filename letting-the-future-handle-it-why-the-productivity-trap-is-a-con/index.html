Letting the Future Handle It

September 2, 2026 AlaskanTF(ATF)
Reflections

We spend our lives running a relay race where no one is ever allowed to stop and sit down for the feast. Here is why it's time to step off the treadmill and just be.

From the moment we're handed our first set of expectations, society gives us a very specific script: sacrifice the present, optimize your routine, and grind today so you can secure a better tomorrow. Study hard now so you can get into a good school. Get into a good school so you can get a good job. Get a good job so you can make more money. Make more money so you can buy a house, save for retirement, and eventually enjoy life.

The problem is that "eventually" always seems to be somewhere further down the road.

We treat the current moment like a glorified construction site for a future that is always just out of reach. There is always something else to prepare for, something else to improve, something else that needs to be secured before we can finally allow ourselves to relax.

And I'm starting to wonder if that "finally" ever actually fucking comes.

The Never-Ending Future

I think one of the biggest traps we've collectively fallen into is the idea that happiness is something that happens later.

We're constantly preparing for some future version of ourselves. Future me will have more money. Future me will have more free time. Future me will finally be able to travel, relax, enjoy hobbies, or just stop worrying about everything.

But when that future finally arrives, it becomes the present. Then we start preparing for another future.

Today's version of you sacrifices for tomorrow's version of you. Tomorrow arrives, and that version of you decides to sacrifice for the version of you ten years from now. The cycle repeats itself, often without us even noticing.

It's basically a relay race where everyone keeps passing the baton forward but nobody is allowed to stop running.

At some point, someone has to ask whether we're actually going anywhere.

Because if we spend our entire lives preparing to live, there is a very real possibility that we simply never get around to it.

The Productivity Trap

This is where I think the modern obsession with productivity becomes bloody ridiculous.

Everything needs to be optimized. Morning routines, your sleep, your diet, your career, your finances, your exercise, and increasingly, even your free time.

You can't just play games anymore. Apparently you should be learning something from them. You can't just watch a movie because you enjoy it, apparently you should be watching something educational or meaningful. You can't spend an afternoon doing nothing because you're "wasting time".

Even resting has become another productivity strategy. You're not resting because you want to rest. You're "recovering" so you can be productive again tomorrow.

There's nothing wrong with wanting to improve yourself. Better habits, financial planning, exercise and discipline are all useful things. They can genuinely make our lives better.

The problem is when they stop being tools and become the entire philosophy of life.

At some point, you start treating yourself like a machine that needs constant maintenance and optimization. If you're tired, you need a better routine. If you're unhappy, you need better time management. If you're burnt out, perhaps you just need to become more organized and disciplined.

Ever thought maybe you're just tired as fuck?

The Singapore Special

Living in Singapore makes this even more obvious.

There is a very strong culture of constantly trying to improve your position. Better grades, better universities, better jobs, higher salaries, better houses, more investments, more qualifications and more career progression. Future proofing almost becomes a way of life.

And I don't think all of this is necessarily bad. Singapore is expensive. Housing is expensive. Raising a family is expensive. There are very real reasons to think about money and the future.

But there is a point where being responsible starts turning into being permanently anxious.

You aren't necessarily worried that you're doing badly. You're worried that someone else is doing better. Someone got a better job, someone bought a nicer condo, someone's investments are performing better, someone's kid got into a better school, someone went on a nicer holiday.

Suddenly, whatever you have isn't quite enough anymore.

There is always another upgrade and another target. Even when life is objectively going pretty well, you're still thinking about what you should be doing next.

That's pretty exhausting.

When Productivity Becomes a Moral Virtue

I also think we've gradually turned productivity into a measure of whether we're being good human beings.

Being busy is admirable. Being tired means you're working hard. Having a full calendar means you're doing something with your life. Meanwhile, doing nothing is often treated as laziness or on the road to being homeless.

When you're exhausted, you're told to manage your time better. When you're burnt out, you're told to build a better routine. When you're unhappy with your work, you're told to become more disciplined or find a more efficient way of doing it.

The solution is almost always to optimize yourself harder.

There is some truth to this. Sometimes we genuinely do have bad habits that need fixing. Sometimes better time management really would help. But there is a point where we stop asking whether we're managing our lives badly and start asking whether we're simply trying to fit too much life into a system that doesn't leave much room for living.

If you're working twelve hours a day and constantly exhausted, maybe you don't need another productivity system that helps you squeeze another 30 minutes out of your evening.

Maybe you're just tired. Get some rest.

We Keep Sacrificing the Present

The strange thing is that we don't only sacrifice the present for ourselves. We also do it for the people who haven't even arrived yet.

We're constantly told that we need to build a better future for the next generation. We should work harder, save more, build more, grow the economy, raise better children and leave behind a better world.

I understand the sentiment. Obviously, we shouldn't intentionally leave future generations with a burning pile of shit. If we can make things better for the people who come after us, we probably should.

But I think we've taken this idea so far that we've forgotten something rather important:

We are a generation too.

We also get one shot at this.

We have our own lives to live, our own experiences to have, our own relationships to build and our own stupid adventures to go on. Why should our entire generation exist purely as a bridge between the generation before us and the generation after us?

The next generation is going to have its own problems anyway. They'll have different technology, different values, different challenges and probably a bunch of problems we haven't even thought about yet. They'll inherit some of what we built, change some of it, and throw some of it away.

And that's fine.

That's how generations work.

Our parents couldn't perfectly prepare the world for us because they couldn't predict what our world would look like. We can't perfectly prepare it for the people who come after us either.

We can leave them something reasonably good. We can avoid making their lives unnecessarily difficult. We can build useful things, preserve important institutions and pass down whatever knowledge we've managed to acquire.

But we don't need to spend our entire lives trying to solve every problem they might ever have.

Let them deal with some shit themselves.

The Future Generation Deserves Its Own Future

There is something slightly strange about the idea that we should spend our entire lives trying to engineer the perfect future for people who haven't even been born yet.

We assume we know what they'll want. We assume we know what they'll need. We assume that the things we consider important today will still be important thirty or fifty years from now.

We don't know that. No one knows the future.

The next generation will have its own ideas about what a good life looks like. They'll have their own priorities and their own problems to solve. Some of the things we spend enormous amounts of effort preparing for might turn out to be completely irrelevant to them.

That's okay.

Our responsibility isn't to make their lives perfect. It's to live our lives responsibly and leave behind something they can work with.

Then they get to do the same.

Otherwise, we end up with this bizarre cycle where every generation is told to sacrifice for the next generation, which then sacrifices for the generation after that. Everyone is working towards someone else's future while nobody really gets to enjoy their own present.

At some point, we have to stop treating our generation like a stepping stone.

Planning for the Future Isn't the Problem

Before this starts sounding like an argument for everyone to quit their jobs, spend their savings on alcohol, cocaine and escorts, that's obviously not what I'm saying.

Planning for the future is important. Save some money. Have an emergency fund. Invest if you can. Think about retirement. Take care of your health. Work towards things that actually matter to you.

I'm not against any of that.

The problem is when preparation becomes more important than the life you're supposedly preparing for.

There's a difference between saving money because you want financial freedom and saving every possible dollar because you're terrified that you might need it thirty years from now.

There's a difference between working hard because you're building something you care about and working yourself into the ground because you're convinced that happiness begins after retirement.

There's also a difference between having goals and constantly feeling like your current life isn't good enough.

The future should be something we prepare for. It shouldn't be something we sacrifice everything for.

Not Everything Needs an ROI

We've also become incredibly uncomfortable with doing things that have no obvious benefit.

You go for a walk and it needs to count as exercise. You travel and someone asks what you got out of the experience. You play a game and you're somehow wasting your time. You spend an afternoon doing nothing and immediately feel like you could have been doing something more productive.

But not everything needs a return.

A meal doesn't need to make you healthier. A holiday doesn't need to make you more productive. A conversation with a friend doesn't need to expand your network. A video game doesn't need to improve your cognitive abilities.

Sometimes you can just enjoy something.

I think this is one of the things we've lost in our obsession with productivity. We've started looking at every activity through the lens of what it gives us in return, rather than whether we actually enjoyed doing it.

Not everything needs to be an investment.

Some things are worth doing simply because they make being alive a little more enjoyable.

Stop Treating Life Like a Project

The thing I dislike most about this mindset is that it turns life into a project.

You start constantly measuring yourself against some imaginary scoreboard. Am I earning enough? Am I saving enough? Am I exercising enough? Am I learning enough? Am I progressing quickly enough?

And if the answer is no, you feel like you're failing.

But failing at what?

There isn't actually a universal scoreboard for life. Nobody is going to hand you a certificate at 80 years old telling you that you achieved 94% of your potential.

You just lived your life.

Hopefully, you enjoyed some of it.

That's the part I think we tend to forget. Life isn't a productivity challenge where there is some final boss waiting at the end to tell you that you've finally optimized yourself enough.

We're just here for a relatively short amount of time.

And then we're gone.

Let the Future Handle It

The future is going to arrive whether we're ready for it or not. We'll make plans, some will work and some won't. We'll change jobs, move houses, make and lose money, meet people, lose people, and probably change our minds about what we want several times along the way.

The version of you that exists ten years from now might have absolutely no interest in the things you think are important today.

And that's okay.

So yes, plan ahead. Save some money. Build something meaningful. Take reasonable precautions.

But don't make the mistake of treating today as merely an investment into tomorrow.

Because tomorrow is going to become today eventually.

And when it does, I hope you're actually there to experience it.

I don't want to spend my entire life building some hypothetical future where I finally get permission to relax. I don't want our entire generation to spend its best years preparing a perfect world for people who haven't even arrived while forgetting that we're alive right now.

We should make the world better where we reasonaably can. We should care about the people who come after us. We should be responsible with the lives we've been given.

But we should also live them.

Take the holiday. Eat the expensive meal. Play the game. Go out with your friends. Sleep in. Spend an afternoon doing absolutely nothing.

Do something completely useless simply because you enjoy it.

You don't need to justify every fucking moment of your existence.

Because the present isn't something we need to sacrifice in order to earn the future.

The present is the future we were waiting for.

So maybe it's time to stop running for a while.

Sit your ass down.

Eat the damn feast.

And let the future handle itself.

Thanks for reading :D

Now fuck off and do something productive.
