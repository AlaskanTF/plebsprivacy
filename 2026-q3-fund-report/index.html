Q3 Review

The headline number for the year sits at +53% YTD, and the composition of that return is the more interesting story this quarter. Roughly 33% of 2026's gains have come from options premium, meaningfully more than in prior years where directional equity moves did most of the work. Selling premium against existing conviction has quietly become a larger part of how the fund makes money, rather than being a side benefit of holding the positions.

Earlier in the year, six new names were added to the book: Walmart (WMT), Vertiv (VRT), Vistra Energy (VST), ServiceNow (NOW), Exxon (XOM), and Netflix (NFLX) — a mix spanning defensive consumer staples, AI-adjacent infrastructure and power demand, enterprise software, energy, and media/streaming. That diversification work was largely done by the time Q3 rolled around.

Q3 itself didn't produce a new addition to the book. Instead, it was a quarter of showing up consistently and managing the exposure already built rather than chasing new ideas.

Strategy and Execution

With the Q1 - Q3 additions (WMT, VRT, VST, NOW, XOM, NFLX) already in place, no further capital was deployed into new positions during Q3 itself. Instead, the focus shifted to actively managing the existing book:

  • Trimming into strength: When positions reached all-time highs, a portion of the position was trimmed/sold rather than left to run unchecked.
  • Re-entering on pullbacks: Capital freed up from trims was redeployed on weakness, frequently at a lower cost basis than the original entry.
  • Continued premium collection: Option selling remained a consistent source of return throughout the quarter.
  • The Equity Accumulator: Options premium is not left idle. It is re-invested during market pullbacks and localized volatility spikes. Accumulated premiums are systematically deployed to purchase high-conviction underlying equities outright. Once acquired, these positions transition immediately into our covered call and wheel framework, compounding the fund's baseline yield.

The net effect has been a gradual reduction in average cost basis across core holdings, without needing to correctly call exact tops or bottoms, just reacting to price doing what price does.

What Went Right

  • Deliberate diversification: WMT, VRT, VST, NOW, XOM, and NFLX added exposure across staples, power/AI infrastructure, enterprise software, energy, and media, rather than doubling down on a single theme.
  • Discipline over discovery in Q3: Once the new names were in place, capital was left to compound rather than forcing further new trade ideas just for the sake of activity.
  • Consistent premium collection: A third of the year's gains came from income generation rather than price appreciation alone.
  • Cost basis management: Systematic trimming and rebuying lowered average costs across holdings without sacrificing exposure.
  • Margin Clearance Protocol: Real-time margin utilization is strictly bounded to the range of 20-40%. However, margins are cleared and managed aggressively to ensure zero structural exposure to black-swan tail events.
  • Cash Management: Cash in hand is kept at a learn 5%, ensuring maximum capital utilization without compromising the fund's safety margin. Our liquidity is dynamic-derived from continuous premium inflow rather than passive cash drag.

What Could Have Been Better

  • Front-loaded new ideas: With six new names added earlier in the year, Q3 itself was quiet on the "new position" front, will revisit if better opportunities present themselves before year-end.
  • Reporting cadence: Without a single defining event or trade to anchor the quarter, tracking the smaller, active-management wins in real time is harder than tracking one big call.

These are being kept in mind heading into Q4.

Outlook for Q4

Heading into the final stretch of 2026, the outlook remains cautiously optimistic. The plan is to keep the current playbook running: trim into strength, rebuy on pullbacks, and keep collecting premium where it's available, rather than forcing new positions just to have a new story to tell.

The goal remains the same as always, stay risk-aware without being blinded by short-term scares, and let the process do the compounding.

Closing Thoughts

53% YTD, six new names added across the year, and roughly a third of gains coming from premium alone, that's a very different shape of return than prior years, and one I'm comfortable with. Boring and profitable beats interesting and broke.

ALSO, we remain comfortably above the high watermark. Unfortunately for investors and fortunately for me, that means performance fees continue to accrue. All jokes aside, performance fees are only ever taken on profits, never on capital. Thus, our interests remain aligned.
